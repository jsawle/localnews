About Place Finder

Place Finder reads a document, picks out the names of places, and shows how often each one is mentioned on a map, in a table and as a word cloud.

How it works

  • The text is matched against a list of about 35,000 places (a gazetteer): countries, states and provinces, cities and towns with 15,000 or more people, continents, oceans and seas, rivers, lakes, mountains, islands and other physical regions.
  • It ignores words that are probably not places, such as people's names (“George Washington”), names that are part of something else (“New York Times”) and ordinary words at the start of a sentence (“Reading the map…”).
  • When several places share a name (Paris in France or Texas), it uses the rest of the document to choose: other places in the same country or state, and phrases like “Paris, Texas”.
  • Your document stays in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.

Things to check

  • Small villages, neighbourhoods, streets, buildings and parks are not in the gazetteer, so they will not be found.
  • Some matches will be wrong. Untick them in the table, or choose a different place under Which place?
  • Scanned PDFs (pictures of pages) have no text to read. Copy and paste the text instead.

Data and code

  • Countries, states and provinces, physical features, borders and the main cities: Natural Earth, public domain.
  • Other cities and towns with 15,000+ people: GeoNames, CC BY 4.0, via the all-the-cities package.
  • Libraries: D3 (ISC), d3-cloud (BSD), TopoJSON (ISC), Mammoth (BSD-2) for Word files, PDF.js (Apache 2.0) for PDFs.
